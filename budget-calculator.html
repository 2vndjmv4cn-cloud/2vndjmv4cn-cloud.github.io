How the 50/30/20 rule works

The 50/30/20 rule is a simple way to start a budget without tracking every penny. You split the money that actually reaches your bank account into three parts:

  • 50% for needs: rent or mortgage, utilities, groceries, insurance, transportation and minimum debt payments. These are bills you'd still have to pay if you lost your job tomorrow.
  • 30% for wants: eating out, streaming, hobbies, travel and shopping that isn't essential.
  • 20% for savings and extra debt payments: an emergency fund, retirement, and anything you pay on debt above the minimum.

Example

If you take home $4,000 a month, the rule suggests about $2,000 for needs, $1,200 for wants and $800 for savings. If your rent alone is $1,800, needs will run over 50%. That's common in expensive areas. The usual fix is to trim wants first, then adjust the split to something like 60/20/20 until your income or costs change.

Use take-home pay, not salary

Start from what lands in your account after taxes and paycheck deductions. If retirement savings already come out of your paycheck, you can count them toward the 20%.

Questions

Is 50/30/20 right for everyone?

No. It's a starting point. People paying off high-interest debt often move more into the 20%, and people in high-rent cities often need more than 50% for needs.

Do minimum debt payments count as needs?

Yes. Minimum payments are needs. Anything extra you pay to clear debt faster goes in the 20%.
