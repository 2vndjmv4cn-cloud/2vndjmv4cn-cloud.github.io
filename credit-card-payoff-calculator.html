How credit card interest builds up

Card interest is usually quoted as a yearly APR but charged monthly. Roughly, each month's interest is the balance times the APR divided by 12. Your payment covers that interest first, and only the rest lowers the balance.

This calculator adds one month of interest, then subtracts your payment, and repeats until the balance reaches zero. It assumes no new purchases and no fees.

Example

A $5,000 balance at 18% APR with $200 a month takes 32 months to clear. Raise the payment to $300 and it's paid off in 20 months, and the interest drops from about $1,314 to about $797.

Why minimum payments take so long

Minimum payments are often a small percentage of the balance, so most of the payment goes to interest. If your payment barely covers the interest, the balance hardly moves. The calculator warns you when a payment doesn't cover the interest at all.

Questions

Does this match my statement exactly?

Not exactly. Card issuers usually charge interest on your average daily balance, and APRs can change. This gives a close estimate.

What if I have several cards?

Use the debt payoff calculator. It compares the snowball and avalanche methods across all your debts.
