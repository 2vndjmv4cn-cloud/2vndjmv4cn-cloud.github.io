Loan Payment Calculator

See the monthly payment, total interest and a year-by-year payoff table for car loans, personal loans and mortgages.

This calculator is for education and planning only. It is not financial advice. Real loans, cards and accounts can have fees, rate changes and rules this tool doesn't include.

How the payment is worked out

Most car loans, personal loans and fixed-rate mortgages are amortizing: you pay the same amount every month, and each payment covers that month's interest first, then reduces the balance. The standard formula is:

Payment = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)−n)

where P is the amount borrowed, r is the yearly rate divided by 12, and n is the number of monthly payments.

Example

A $20,000 loan at 6% for 5 years (60 payments) comes to about $386.66 a month. Over the full loan you pay about $3,199 in interest. Early payments are mostly interest; by the last year almost all of each payment goes to the balance.

Ways to pay less interest

  • A shorter loan has a higher payment but much less total interest.
  • Even a small extra payment toward the balance shortens the loan, as long as your lender applies it to principal and doesn't charge a prepayment fee.
  • A lower rate matters more on long loans like mortgages.

Questions

Does this include taxes, insurance or fees?

No. For mortgages, property tax, home insurance and PMI are usually added on top. Loans can also have origination fees.

Why is my lender's number slightly different?

Lenders may round each month differently, count days exactly, or include fees in the APR. The difference is usually a few cents to a few dollars.
