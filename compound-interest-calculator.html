Compound Interest Calculator

Enter a starting amount, a monthly deposit and a yearly rate to see how your money could grow, year by year.

This calculator is for education and planning only. It is not financial advice. Real loans, cards and accounts can have fees, rate changes and rules this tool doesn't include.

What compound interest means

Compound interest is interest earned on your interest. Each month the balance grows by a small percentage, and next month's growth is worked out on that bigger balance. Over long periods the growth can end up larger than the money you put in.

How this calculator works

It adds growth monthly (the yearly rate divided by 12) and assumes each deposit is made at the end of the month. The balance after n months is:

Start × (1 + r)n + Deposit × ((1 + r)n − 1) ÷ r

Example

Depositing $100 a month for 30 years at 7% gives about $121,997. You'd have put in $36,000; the rest is growth. The same deposits at 4% give about $69,400, which is why the rate and the number of years matter so much.

Keep expectations realistic

Savings accounts pay a set rate that can change. Investments like index funds go up and down, and past returns don't guarantee future ones. Use a lower rate if you want a cautious estimate.

Questions

What rate should I use?

For a savings account, use its current yearly rate (APY). For long-term investing estimates, many people test a few rates, for example 4%, 6% and 8%, to see a range.

Does this include taxes or inflation?

No. Growth in a regular account may be taxed, and inflation reduces what the money will buy in the future.
